Editorial standards

The rules you can hold us to

A values claim is worthless unless readers can check it. These are our standing rules, in public, so you can.

Quotes and character

We do not cut, truncate or reframe a subject's quotes to change their meaning. If a quote must be trimmed for length, the trim cannot change what the person meant; when in doubt, we run the full context.

We do not run stories that attack a subject's character. Disagreement with a product, an argument or a company is fair game. Commentary on a person's character, motives or worth is not. Attack the argument, not the person.

This rule exists because people on our team have been on the wrong end of outlets that did otherwise. It is not open for revision.

Data and opinion stay separate

Every review has a data section built only from measurements and a verdict section built from judgment. The two never share a paragraph. Each metric gets a labeled 1 to 10 score next to its number; we publish no overall score. How we test.

Sponsorship and affiliate disclosure

Our revenue comes from affiliate links and clearly labeled sponsored content. We run no display ads and no membership. Money can fund coverage. Money cannot shape coverage.

  • Sponsored content is labeled as sponsored above the fold, never in fine print.
  • A sponsor gets no review approval, no editorial input and no veto over any piece.
  • Affiliate links can appear in genuine reviews. The verdict is written before any retailer link is chosen, and commission rates never influence it.
  • A sponsor we also cover editorially gets the same treatment as any other subject.

Community sentiment

We watch where makers are talking. A trending thread is a lead, not a story. Before "the community thinks" appears in print we confirm the sentiment is broad rather than a few loud accounts, cross-check other channels, ask the company or person involved for comment, and ask whether there is a real news angle at all. We do not quote or screenshot individual forum users as if they speak for everyone.

Corrections

When we get something wrong we fix it in place and say what changed and when, at the top of the article. [Corrections policy wording to be finalized before launch.]

Comments and accounts

No anonymous comments. Accounts come from Google, Discord or an email link. A new account completes a second verification 1 to 24 hours after signup; until then its first comment is held from public view. Once verified, comments post instantly. This is an anti-astroturfing measure against burner-email drive-bys, not a paywall and not a popularity filter. Staff accounts are labeled. We remove comments that break the community standards in our terms and we say so when we do.
